Courses

Wheels, tracks and rollers

W, T and R are three separate endorsements on your driver licence, often done together and known on site as WTR. You need the matching one to drive a special-type machine on a road, and a road is broader than you might think: it can include any place the public can get to.

Wheels endorsement

For machines that run on wheels, other than forklifts, tractors and the other vehicle types NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi (NZTA) lists separately.

Machines likeWheel loaders, skid steer loaders, graders and wheeled excavators

Tracks endorsement

For machines that run on self-laying tracks. Tracked diggers come under this one.

Machines likeTracked excavators, crawler tractors and crawler loaders

Rollers endorsement

For machines that run on rollers, the compaction gear you see on roading and site work.

Machines likeDouble drum vibratory rollers, multi-tyred rollers and steel wheeled compactors

What the training covers

From the pre-start check to parking up

  • Theory: the machine, the legal rules for driving it on a road, and safe driving.
  • Pre-start checks before you turn the key.
  • Practical driving: driving and turning, reversing, then parking and shutting down.
  • Safety first, the whole way through.

After the course you take your course certificate to a driver licensing agent to have the endorsement added to your licence. The certificate on its own does not let you drive on the road.

Questions

Before you book

Do I need a full licence first?

You need a full class 1 (car) licence before you can apply for a W, T or R endorsement. You can do the course assessment while you are on a restricted licence, but you can only apply for the endorsement once your full class 1 comes through.

Is WTR one endorsement or three?

Three. W is for wheels, T for tracks and R for rollers. WTR is just the shorthand for getting all three. Each one goes on your licence separately, and NZTA charges its own application fee for each (as at October 2026 that was $62.10 per endorsement; check NZTA's licence fees page for the current amount). NZTA's fee is separate from the course fee.

How much is a course and how long does it take?

Ring Mandeep on 021 187 1831 or send an email. Tell him which endorsements you need and when you can get time off, and he can talk you through the price and the dates.

What happens after the course?

You take your course certificate to a driver licensing agent with your ID, fill in the endorsement application form (DL19) and do an eyesight check. Once the endorsement is on your licence you can drive that type of machine on the road, as long as your licence class also covers the machine (that depends on its weight and, for wheeled machines, how fast you drive it). The certificate on its own is not enough.

Do I have to renew my endorsements?

Not separately. W, T and R endorsements expire on the same date as your driver licence and renew automatically when you renew it, with no extra fee. The catch: if one has been expired for more than five years, you have to do the course again.
